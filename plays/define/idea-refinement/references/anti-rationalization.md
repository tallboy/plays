# Anti-Rationalization: Idea Refinement

**Back to:** [Idea Refinement](../SKILL.md)

---

## Why Developers Skip This Skill

| Excuse | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| "I'll figure it out as I code" | Rework costs 10x more than upfront clarity |
| "The requirements seem obvious" | Different people interpret "obvious" differently |
| "This is blocking progress" | Implementing the wrong thing blocks progress more |
| "We can iterate based on feedback" | Iteration requires a clear baseline to iterate FROM |
| "Stakeholder will tell me if it's wrong" | Stakeholders don't know it's wrong until they see it (too late) |

---

## Cost of Skipping

### Scenario: Developer Skips Idea Refinement

**What they did:**
- Started coding based on brief Slack message
- Made assumptions about scope and requirements
- Built complete feature over 3 days

**What happened:**
1. **Day 1-3:** Implementation feels smooth
2. **Day 4:** Demo to stakeholder
3. **Day 4 afternoon:** "That's not what I meant at all"
4. **Day 5-7:** Complete rewrite after clarification
5. **Total time:** 7 days

**If they HAD used idea refinement:**
1. **Day 1 morning:** 30-minute refinement session, clarified requirements
2. **Day 1 afternoon - Day 3:** Implementation (correct approach)
3. **Day 4:** Demo to stakeholder: "Perfect!"
4. **Total time:** 4 days

**Savings:** 3 days (43% faster)

## Real-World Evidence

### Study: IBM System Sciences Institute
- Cost to fix requirements errors:
  - **During requirements phase:** $1
  - **During design:** $5
  - **During implementation:** $10
  - **During testing:** $20
  - **After deployment:** $200

**Conclusion:** Requirements clarification has 200x ROI.

### Survey: Standish Group CHAOS Report
- 37% of project failures attributed to "unclear requirements"
- Projects with clear requirements have **3x higher success rate**

## Team Impact

### When One Developer Skips Idea Refinement

**Effect on team:**
- Code reviews become requirements debates (wrong venue)
- Frequent pivots disrupt team's understanding
- Knowledge gaps form (assumptions not documented)
- Stakeholder trust erodes ("they don't listen to us")

**Compounding effect:**
- Team culture shifts to "just build something"
- Quality drops across entire codebase
- Product-market fit suffers

---

## When It's Actually OK to Skip

There are legitimate cases where idea refinement isn't needed:

| Scenario | Alternative Approach |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Bug fix with clear reproduction** | Skip to debugging and fix directly |
| **Requirements already in spec** | Use existing spec, proceed to planning |
| **Trivial change** | For changes < 30 min, brief mental check is sufficient |
| **Emergency hotfix** | Fix immediately, document after |

**Key distinction:** These are exceptions, not the rule. Default is always refine first.
